The history of personal computing is really a history of a handful of stubborn people. Alan Turing laid the theoretical groundwork long before the machines existed. Decades later, Steve Wozniak built a computer in a garage that Steve Jobs was determined to sell to the world. Meanwhile, Bill Gates and Paul Allen bet that software, not hardware, would matter most. Companies like IBM, Microsoft, and Apple traded the lead back and forth for years. Grace Hopper gave us the compiler and, along the way, a memorable story about an actual moth stuck in a relay. It is easy to forget how young this whole field really is, and how much of it grew out of pure curiosity.
